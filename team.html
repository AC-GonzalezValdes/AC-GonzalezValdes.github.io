Past Members

Vivian Chavez (she/they)

CUNY Climate Scholar and Honors Thesis Student, 2025–2026 · Lehman College

How nutrient limitation affects carbon storage and sinking in the ocean microbe Prochlorococcus

Vivian explored how low-nutrient conditions of the Sargasso Sea influenced Prochlorococcus sinking rate, biofilm aggregation, and how it could inform Prochlorococcus-facilitated particulate organic carbon export. This project began as part of the 2025–26 CUNY Climate Scholars, and continued as their senior honors thesis. Vivian graduated with a B.S. in Interdisciplinary Environmental Science at Lehman College in 2026. Her research interests involve microbial ecology related to the oceanic carbon cycle.

Now: C-CoMP Bridge-to-PhD Postbaccalaureate Fellow, Stony Brook University

Mariam Moazzam (she/her)

Susan Locke Interdisciplinary Course Student (Spring 2025) and Independent Study Student (Fall 2025)

How nutrient limitation affects aggregation and EPS production in the ocean microbe Prochlorococcus

Mariam Moazzam conducted independent research in the Gonzalez-Nayeck Lab, studying ocean carbon export and how nutrient limitation influences Prochlorococcus aggregation and extracellular polymeric substance (EPS) production in the Sargasso Sea, with implications for carbon sequestration. During the Susan Locke Interdisciplinary course, she also visited Columbia University’s Lamont-Doherty Earth Observatory, where she explored tree cores and dendrochronology research. Since then, Mariam has worked with Residual Carbon, a carbon project developer, and on data engineering and carbon modeling projects at Tufts University and in Costa Rica focused on agroforestry and reforestation. She is now pursuing a master’s degree in Technology Management at Columbia University and hopes to continue working at the intersection of sustainability, data, and technology.

Now: M.S. Student in Technology Management, Columbia University; Data Engineer and Carbon Researcher, Tufts University

Liam Cooper (he/him)

Independent Study Student (Spring 2024) and Honors Thesis Student (2024–2025)

Sugar-related uncertainties in a proxy for ancient CO2

For his honors thesis, Liam researched if there is a carbon isotope fractionation associated with sugar excreted by cyanobacteria, thereby potentially impacting a proxy for ancient atmospheric CO2. After graduating from Baruch, he continued his work at LDEO, this time researching how climate-driven shifts in phytoplankton carbon fixation may alter the strength of the biological pump and the ocean’s overall capacity to buffer future anthropogenic climate changes. He is interested in environmental and climate law & policy, specifically within built and urban environments!

Now: J.D. Candidate, UC Berkeley School of Law
